Laws of Integer Indices

An index tells us how many times to multiply by a base. What happens when the index reaches zero, or becomes negative?

01 · Build the idea

Why is the zero index 1?

21 = 2. One step left divides by 2, giving 20 = 1. This works for any non-zero base.

Why do negative indices make fractions?

Take another step left: 2−1 = 12. A negative index means a reciprocal, not a negative answer.

02 · Use the laws

Seven laws to know

Check the base first. The first three laws act on powers with the same base.

01

Multiply powers

am×an=am+n

Same base: add the indices.

23 × 22 = 25 = 32

02

Divide powers

aman=am−n

Same non-zero base: subtract the indices.

3532 = 33 = 27

03

Power of a power

(am)n=am×n

Multiply the indices.

(23)2 = 26 = 64

04

Power of a product

(ab)n=anbn

Apply the index to both factors.

(2 × 3)2 = 22 × 32 = 36

05

Zero index

a0=1

For any non-zero base.

70 = 1

06

Negative index

a−n=1an

Take the reciprocal; the base must be non-zero.

2−3 = 18

07

Power of a quotient

(ab)n=anbn

Apply the index to the numerator and denominator.

(23)2=49

Here m and n are integers. Every denominator must be non-zero, and a base with a negative index must be non-zero. The zero-index law requires a ≠ 0; 00 is not defined here.

03 · Check your thinking

Two common traps

Do not add powers by adding indices

23 + 22 = 8 + 4 = 12, not 25. The index law applies to multiplication, not addition.

Watch the brackets around a negative base

(−2)2 = 4, but −22 = −4. Without brackets, only the 2 is squared.
