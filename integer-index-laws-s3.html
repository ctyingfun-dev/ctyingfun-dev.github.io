When do we add, subtract or multiply indices?

23 = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8: the index counts three factors of 2. What happens when we multiply two powers, divide them, or raise a whole power to another power?

First, the laws of indices

Add, subtract or multiply the indices?

01 · Multiply powers

How many factors are there altogether?

am×an=am+n

Same base: add the indices.

23 × 22 = 25 = 32

Three factors of 2, followed by two more: five in total. We add the indices because we are multiplying the powers.

02 · Divide powers

Which factors are left after division?

aman=am−n

Same non-zero base: subtract the indices.

3532 = 33 = 27

Two of the five factors of 3 divide out, leaving three. A zero denominator would make the division undefined.

03 · Power of a power

How many groups of three 2s?

(am)n=am×n

Multiply the indices.

(23)2 = 26 = 64

The outer index repeats the whole bracket twice. Two groups of three factors give six factors, not five.

04 · Power of a product

Does the index act on both factors?

(ab)n=anbn

Apply the index to both factors.

(2 × 3)2 = 22 × 32 = 36

05 · Power of a quotient

Do we raise just the numerator?

(ab)n=anbn

Apply the index to the numerator and denominator.

(23)2=49

Here m and n are integers. Use each law only when all the powers and fractions are defined; every denominator must be non-zero. Start with positive indices, then extend the pattern below.

Now extend the pattern: zero and negative indices

What should 20 and 2−1 mean?

We have used division to subtract indices. Start at 23 = 8, then reduce the index by one each time. The next values are 4, then 2. What happens when we reach zero and continue?

Why 1, rather than 0?

21 = 2. One step left divides by 2, giving 20 = 1. This works for any non-zero base.

Does a negative index mean a negative answer?

Take another step left: 2−1 = 12. A negative index means a reciprocal, not a negative answer.

Can you explain the step you just made?

One smaller index. One division by the base.

The “number of factors” explanation starts with a positive integer index. For zero and negative indices, we extend the same multiplication-and-division pattern. A reciprocal is not a change of sign.
