Percentages

From percentage change to compound growth: always identify the original amount.

01 · Foundations

The original amount is 100%

A percentage is a fraction out of 100. For example, 35% = 35 ÷ 100 = 0.35, and 0.08 = 8%.

To express one amount as a percentage of another, divide by the reference amount, then multiply by 100%.

18 out of 24 = (18 ÷ 24) × 100% = 75%

Percentage changenew − originaloriginal × 100%

A positive result means an increase; a negative result means a decrease. The original amount must be non-zero.

The expression in brackets is the multiplier. A 15% increase gives 1.15; a 15% decrease gives 0.85.

“Increase by 20%” means reach 120% of the original. “Increase to 120%” means the same thing. “Decrease to 20%” means an 80% decrease.

02 · Work backwards

Reverse percentages

Rewind a percentage change

After a 15% decrease, an amount is HK$612. What was it before?

Original amount?
× 0.85→
Final amount612

The final amount is 85% of the original. Reverse the multiplier to find the missing 100%.

When the final amount is given, divide by the multiplier to recover the original amount.

O = Nmultiplier

After an increase of r%: divide by (1 + r%).
After a decrease of r%: divide by (1 − r%).

Do not apply the same percentage to the final amount. It has a different base. After a 100% decrease, the multiplier is zero, so the original cannot be recovered from the final amount alone.

Example 1 · Before the sale

A jacket costs HK$612 after a 15% discount. Find its marked price.

  1. Remaining percentage = 100% − 15% = 85%.
  2. Let the marked price be HK$x. Then 0.85x = 612.
  3. x = 612 ÷ 0.85 = 720.

Marked price = HK$720. Check: 720 × 0.85 = 612.

03 · More than one change

Multiply the multipliers

Predict before you calculate

An amount starts at 100, rises by 20%, then falls by 20%. Where does it finish?

Original amount100

Make a prediction to reveal the first change.

When an amount changes twice, the second percentage uses the amount after the first change. Find that intermediate amount before continuing.

Example 2 · Two price changes

A bicycle costs HK$2,000. Its price rises by 10%, then falls by 15%.

Step 1 · Increase the original price by 10%

Base for this change: HK$2,000

2,000 × (1 + 10%) = HK$2,200

The increase is 10% of HK$2,000, which is HK$200.

Step 2 · Decrease the new price by 15%

Base for this change: HK$2,200, not HK$2,000

2,200 × (1 − 15%) = HK$1,870

The decrease is 15% of HK$2,200, which is HK$330.

Step 3 · Compare the final price with the original

Base for the overall change: HK$2,000

1,870 − 2,0002,000 × 100% = −6.5%

Overall: a 6.5% decrease. The HK$200 rise and HK$330 fall give a net decrease of HK$130.

Shortcut: multiply the two multipliers2,000 × 1.10 × 0.85 = 1,870

Do not subtract the rates to get 10% − 15% = −5%. The two percentages use different bases.

Why do a 20% rise and a 20% fall not cancel?

100 × 1.20 = 120
120 × 0.80 = 96

The rise adds 20, but the fall subtracts 24. The final amount is 4% below the original.

What rise reverses a 20% fall?

A 20% fall leaves 0.8 of the original. The recovery multiplier is 1 ÷ 0.8 = 1.25, so a 25% rise is needed.

Simple and compound interest

Simple interest

Interest is calculated on the original principal only.

I = P × r% × t
A = P(1 + r% × t)

HK$5,000 at 4% p.a. for 3 years:

I = 5,000 × 0.04 × 3 = HK$600
A = 5,000 + 600 = HK$5,600

“p.a.” means per annum (per year). For simple interest over 9 months, use t = 9/12.

Compound interest

Interest is added to the balance and earns interest in later periods.

A = P(1 + r%)t
I = A − P

HK$5,000 at 4% p.a., compounded annually for 3 years:

A = 5,000(1.04)3 = HK$5,624.32
I = 5,624.32 − 5,000 = HK$624.32

The annual formula assumes a whole number of annual compounding periods. Keep full precision during working and round money to the nearest cent at the end.

Extension: compounding more than once a year

For a nominal annual rate of r%, compounded m times per year, the rate per period is (rm)% and the number of periods is mt.

A = P(1 + (rm)%)mt

HK$8,000 at 6% p.a., compounded half-yearly for 2 years: m = 2, so A = 8,000(1.03)4 ≈ HK$9,004.07.

05 · Repeated percentage change

Growth and depreciation

A laptop through time

Purchase price: HK$12,000. Depreciation: 15% of the remaining value each year.

Year 0

HK$12,000.00

100% of the purchase price remains.

At purchase, no depreciation has occurred.

Let V0 be the initial value and V the value after n periods, each with a constant percentage rate r%.

GrowthV = V0(1 + r%)n
DepreciationV = V0(1 − r%)n

For depreciation in value, 0 ≤ r ≤ 100. The same multiplier is applied each period, but the amount lost changes.

A 10% annual depreciation does not mean a 30% loss after 3 years. The remaining fraction is 0.93 = 0.729, so the loss is 27.1%.

Example 3 · A laptop's value

A laptop costs HK$12,000 and depreciates by 15% each year. Find its value after 3 years.

V = 12,000(1 − 0.15)3
= 12,000(0.85)3
= HK$7,369.50

Total depreciation = 12,000 − 7,369.50 = HK$4,630.50.

Find an unknown annual growth rate

A population grows from 20,000 to 24,200 in 2 years at a constant annual rate of r%.

(1 + r%)2 = 24,200 ÷ 20,000 = 1.21
1 + r% = √1.21 = 1.1
r% = 10%
r = 10

The annual growth rate is 10%.

06 · Apply your knowledge

Practice

Give monetary answers to the nearest cent where necessary.

Revision task

A student deposits HK$4,000 at 5% p.a., compounded annually for 2 years. Find the final balance. The student uses the entire balance to buy a camera, which then depreciates by 10% per year for 2 years. Find the camera's value and its overall percentage change from the original HK$4,000.

Full solution

Final balance = 4,000 × (1 + 5%)2 = HK$4,410.

Camera's value = 4,410 × (1 − 10%)2 = HK$3,572.10.

Overall percentage change = (3,572.10 − 4,000) ÷ 4,000 × 100% = −10.6975%.

This is a 10.7% decrease, correct to 1 decimal place. The depreciation starts from HK$4,410, while the overall change uses HK$4,000 as the base.

Final check: identify the base, choose the multiplier, write the equation, and check whether the answer is reasonable. A change from 40% to 50% is 10 percentage points, but the relative increase is (10 ÷ 40) × 100% = 25%.
